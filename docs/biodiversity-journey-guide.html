01

Your first move is curiosity.

You can try the learning activity before creating an account. Start with a question, then choose how you want to explore.

Try it freely

Find the journey

If the welcome screen is showing, choose Continue exploring to reach Explore.

Explore→See my journey

Scroll to “Small visitor. Big connection.” Read the question and choose an answer. If your idea needs adjusting, the explanation helps you try again.

This is practice. Answers do not award Pollen or become saved learning checkpoints. Returning later may start the question again.

Keep your field guide

Sign in when you want to save

Use Sign in on the journey page to save plants and track your app journey. The path helps you find a useful next action.

Look for “Try next” beside a checkpoint. The home card also suggests where to go next.

This guide uses the app’s English labels. Other app languages use translated labels.

What counts as progress? The path records things you do in the app: exploring a plant, saving one, and making check-ins. It is not a test score or a measurement of your neighborhood’s biodiversity.
02

Three checkpoints. Your own pace.

Use Your field guide path to see your place in the journey. These checkpoints can be explored in any order.

Missing the check-in button? Some saved plants do not support check-ins yet. At the Notice what changes step, the journey offers Choose a plant for check-ins if none of your saved plants support them. Choose a built-in plant such as Sunflower, save it, and look for “I checked this plant.” Writing an ordinary note is not the same as making this check-in.
03

Follow the pollen.

Pollinators connect plants to the next generation. Here is one way a small visit can matter.

Try explaining it: How could fewer flower visitors affect the next generation of plants? Use the words pollen, seed, and plant.

04

One plant. Three chances to notice.

Here is an example adventure. Choose days that work for you, and a place you are allowed to visit.

1
Monday · Meet the plant

Start with one question.

Open Sunflower from search and save it. Read its visitor list. Observe the same species you saved, at school or in a park. If no sunflower is nearby, choose another supported species you can actually observe and save that plant instead.

“Which visitors arrive?”

When you have actually checked the plant, use I checked this plant. A saved profile alone is not an outdoor observation.

2
Thursday · Look again

Compare, don’t guess.

Notice flowers, weather, or visitors. It is okay to see no animals. Make another check-in on this different day.

“Today is different because…”

If you want a written record, add a note on the saved plant’s page. The note and the check-in serve different purposes.

3
Next Tuesday · Connect

Tell the plant’s story.

Check once more, then look at your journey. Three different check-in days complete Notice what changes.

“What might help these visitors?”

There is no penalty for the days between visits. After the checkpoints, choose Explore another plant and keep asking questions.

Your best reward is an observation you can explain. A check-in records that you checked. It does not verify that a pollinator arrived, identify every visitor, or prove a change in biodiversity.
05

A little reward for exploring.

Pollen adds a playful way to personalize the app. Your field guide and your questions matter more than collecting points.

First discovery of a species+5 Pollen

Open a new built-in plant species from search while signed in. Reopening the same species does not keep earning the first-discovery reward.

Today’s bee-plant quest+20 Pollen

For “Today: find a plant that helps bees,” open a built-in plant from search whose visitors include Bee. This quest rewards once per day; Sunflower is one example.

Make your avatar yours

See my journey→Pollen & avatars

You arrive at Quests & avatar. Under Make it yours, choose a style. Use Buy to unlock it with Pollen, or Equip to wear one you already own.

  • ✳Sprout0 Pollen
  • ☀Sunshine25 Pollen
  • ✿Meadow60 Pollen
  • ✧Pollen guardian120 Pollen

Style names and prices above match the app. These small guide symbols are illustrations, not screenshots of the actual avatars. Spending Pollen never lowers your level.

Read XP without rushing

Reward Pollen also adds XP until you gain one level that day. At 100 XP, your level rises and XP resets to 0. Extra XP does not carry over.

Further rewards that day still add Pollen, but no more XP. XP can grow again the next day.

Example: A first Sunflower discovery can earn 5 Pollen plus today’s 20-Pollen quest: 25 Pollen total. Buying Sunshine spends the 25 Pollen; it does not erase the XP you earned.

Seeing zero XP after leveling up is expected. It is not lost progress.

Other rewards, and why you never need to chase them

Check-ins: a supported plant’s check-in can earn 5 Pollen per plant per day. Repeating that check-in on the same day does not add more. The journey counts distinct days, not button presses.

Optional planting: eligible planting records can earn 25 Pollen, or 50 for a catalog plant with supported pollinator value. Rewards are limited to three eligible species per day and once per species. Extra planting records get no Pollen, including on later days; there is no reward carryover.

Plant only when it makes sense locally. You never need to plant anything to finish the three-checkpoint journey. The app also has rewards for eligible pollinator identifications and planting photos; they are optional. A reward is not proof that a species is suitable for your area.

06

Think beyond one flower.

A variety of locally native plants that flower at different times can support different pollinators. Start by noticing what already grows nearby.

Observe gently

Watch from a respectful distance. Stay on permitted paths, avoid disturbing animals or nests, and ask an adult before changing a planting area.

You can use a school garden, a park, or a place you already visit. Write down what you actually notice—even “no visitors during my observation.”

Check what is local

The app’s broad region choices are North Carolina, USA, Another region, and Skip · no region. They do not provide a complete local suitability check.

Before planting, use a local native-plant guide with an adult. A plant that helps pollinators somewhere may be unsuitable or invasive elsewhere.

The app excludes Chinese wisteria from planting rewards. Other rewarded plants still need a local check.

Aa

A few useful words.

You do not need to memorize a dictionary. Use these words to tell the story of what you see.

Biodiversity
The variety of living things, including different kinds of plants and animals.
Pollinator
An animal that moves pollen between flowers. Bees are one example; other insects, birds, and some mammals can also pollinate.
Pollination
The transfer of pollen to the flower part that receives it. It can help a plant produce seeds.
Locally native plant
A plant that occurs naturally in your region. “Native” depends on where you are.
Check-in
The app action that records you checked a supported saved plant. It is separate from saving the plant or adding an ordinary note.
Pollen, XP, and level
Game rewards and app progression. Real pollen is part of plant reproduction; game Pollen is a currency for avatars.
For teachers & families

Ask for a story, not a score.

  • “What did you notice, and what are you still unsure about?”
  • “How might the same plant look different on another day?”
  • “Can you explain the flower → pollen → seed connection?”
  • “What would we need to find out before planting this here?”

Offer indoor research or a shared class observation when outdoor access is difficult. The practice question does not store mastery, and the journey checks app actions. Assess understanding through discussion, sketches, or a short explanation rather than Pollen totals.

There is room for real life.

Your exploration can be small, uneven, and full of questions.

“I don’t have a garden.”

My Garden is also a field guide. Save a plant you want to learn about. A school or park observation can be a starting point; planting is optional.

“I didn’t see any visitors.”

That is a real observation. Record what you saw, along with details such as the time or weather. One quiet visit does not mean pollinators never come.

“I forgot a day.”

Come back when you can. The checkpoint uses three different days, with no consecutive-day requirement and no streak to lose.

“I answered the question wrong.”

Read the explanation and try another answer. There is no point penalty. Explaining why an answer changed is part of learning.

“My saved plant has no check-in.”

Some species are outside the built-in guide. Use Choose a plant for check-ins, then save a supported plant such as Sunflower. Look for I checked this plant.

“My progress won’t load.”

Unknown progress is not zero progress. Try Refresh journey when your connection returns. You can still practice and explore, but wait for successful saving before assuming a check-in was recorded.

“The path is complete. Now what?”

Use Explore another plant. Compare flowers, visitors, or blooming times. The three checkpoints introduce a habit; your questions can keep growing.

“Where are my avatars?”

From Explore, choose See my journey, then Pollen & avatars. The destination is titled Quests & avatar. Sign in to see your saved rewards.
