# Your neighborhood nature adventure

**[Open the illustrated guide](biodiversity-journey-guide.html)** for the full tutorial with original vector illustrations. Open the HTML file in a browser; the guide works offline. Account activity in the app needs its normal services.

Your mission is to get to know a plant, notice its visitors, and return to see what changes. You do not need a garden or a camera.

## Start with curiosity

1. From the welcome page, choose **Continue exploring**.
2. On Explore, choose **See my journey**.
3. Try the pollination challenge. A bee moving pollen between flowers of the same species can help the plant produce seeds. Incorrect answers give you an explanation and another try.

The challenge is practice. It does not award Pollen or save a mastery score. You can try it before signing in.

## Follow your field guide path

Sign in when you want to keep plants and track your journey. The three checkpoints are independent; explore them in any order.

### 1. Explore a plant

Choose **Explore a bee-visited plant**, then open a plant from the search results. Sunflower is a useful example. Read about its flower visitors.

A recorded discovery completes this checkpoint. Opening a profile through search earns discovery credit; a direct species link does not. This records exploration, not proof that you have seen the plant outdoors or learned every fact.

### 2. Save a plant

Choose **Save plant** on its profile. **My Garden** is your personal field guide: you can save a plant you observe without owning or planting it. Saving itself does not earn Pollen.

This checkpoint reflects your current saved collection. Deleting every saved plant reopens it; other completed checkpoints remain.

### 3. Notice what changes

Open a saved plant. Look for **Notice what changed**, then use **I checked this plant** after checking it. Look for leaves, flowers, or visitors. Checking does not mean watering.

Record check-ins on **three different days**. They do not need to be consecutive. Several plants on one day still count as one day toward the checkpoint.

Some identified plants lack this control. If none of your saved plants has a check-in control, Journey offers **Choose a plant for check-ins**. Choose and save a supported plant, such as sunflower.

## Understand your rewards

Pollen is game currency for avatar styles. It is not real pollen, money, or a measurement of biodiversity.

| Activity | Pollen | What counts |
|---|---:|---|
| Explore a new supported catalog species | 5 | Once per species |
| Today's bee-plant quest | 20 | Open a qualifying plant through search; once per day |
| Check an eligible saved plant | 5 | Once per plant per day |
| Save a plant or answer the practice question | 0 | Learning actions without currency awards |

A first sunflower discovery can earn **5 + 20 = 25 Pollen**. Reopening it does not earn another first-species reward. The daily quest can be completed again on a later day.

Open **See my journey → Pollen & avatars** to choose a style: Sprout is free, Sunshine costs 25, Meadow costs 60, and Pollen guardian costs 120. Buying spends your balance without lowering your level.

XP tracks your level separately. Reward Pollen adds XP until you gain one level that day. At 100 XP, the level rises and XP resets to zero; overflow is not carried forward. Further rewards that day add Pollen only. Taking a break does not lose a streak.

## Try a three-visit adventure

Choose a supported plant you can actually revisit; sunflower is only an example. Make check-ins on the saved profile that matches the plant you observe.

- **First visit:** explore that species through search, save it, and check the matching plant. First exploration and the bee quest can earn 25 Pollen; the check-in adds 5.
- **Another day:** check the plant. Are flowers open? Any visitors? No visitors is a useful observation too.
- **A third day:** check again and compare what you noticed. The third distinct day completes the observation checkpoint.

Ask: *What might change with the weather, season, or time of day?* The check-in records that you checked; use a notebook or the plant's note feature for details.

## Connect to your neighborhood

Different locally native plants, flowering at different times, can support different pollinators. Observe respectfully. Do not touch or capture visitors to earn points.

Planting is optional. Ask an adult and consult a local plant guide first. The app's regional guidance currently covers North Carolina only. A plant attractive to pollinators is not automatically appropriate everywhere.

If you actually planted a suitable plant, **I planted this** logs it. Eligible planting earns 25 Pollen, or 50 for a catalog plant with supported pollinator value: once per species and up to three eligible species per day. Extra logs receive no planting reward later. Chinese wisteria earns no planting reward because local suitability is unverified. Planting is never required for the core journey.

## When something is unclear

- **No garden?** Observe a permitted school, community, or neighborhood plant, or try the free learning challenge indoors.
- **No visitors?** That is an observation, not a failure.
- **Missed a day?** Continue when you can. The three days are not a streak.
- **Progress unavailable?** Choose **Refresh journey**. Missing data does not mean lost progress.
- **Not signed in?** Explore and practice freely; sign in for saved plants and account progress. Practice answers do not carry into your account.

For teachers: ask students to explain pollen transfer and seeds, compare two observation days, and name a local source they would consult before planting. Points indicate app activity, not assessed understanding or ecological impact.
